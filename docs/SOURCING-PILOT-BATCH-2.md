## 2. The import dependency, from the registry

**Indonesia's import dependency is settled by the food regulator's own
registration, not by a brand name.** A product registered `MD` with
**no importer** is made by an Indonesian company; `ML` with an importer
ID is made abroad.

**The registry's search matches cosmetics as well as food.** The
`tamarind` query returns body scrubs and perfumes; only the
`MD`/`ML` registrations are food. **Every count below filters to
`MD`/`ML` first.**

## 6. How this document was checked

- **Every cited URL was fetched.** The prices were read from the
  **target product's own price block on its own page** — Migros's
  "2 Franken 90" format, Asia Shop's "2,49€inkl. MwSt. + Grundpreis"
  format, Tokopedia's "Subtotal Rp…" block.
- **The related-product trap was caught TWICE — once by the
  researcher and once by me.**
  The Spice House "tamarind" search returned four unrelated trending
  products with their prices; those prices are **not** in the table,
  and the US tamarind row is held back rather than filled with them.
  And in the **Swiss** rows the first extraction took **CHF 2.80** for
  the coconut milk — which was the **related-products carousel's**
  Thai Kitchen 250 ml, not the page's own product. The page's own
  product is **"Migros · Kokosnussmilch, 400 ml, CHF 1.40"**, which
  is the own-brand line, and it is both **cheaper** and a *different
  product*. **That is exactly the trap the contract warns about, and
  it is worth recording that the trap caught the second reader too.**
  The extractor now takes the price block immediately **before** the
  product's own heading, never one from the carousel below it.
- **Search-page ranges are marked as ranges**, and are never presented
  as a product's price.
- **Stock state is recorded where the page states it**: two of the
  three German tamarind pages say "Nicht vorrätig".
- **The import dependency comes from the registry**, and every count
  filters to the `MD`/`ML` food registrations first.
- **The research was done by one researcher** and the figures were
  re-read from the fetched pages afterwards, by the second pass. The
  **US** rows are the ones that did not survive that process intact:
  they are held back.
- **The second pass corrected the first pass in three places**, and
  all three are recorded above rather than quietly overwritten: the
  **Migros coconut-milk price** (the carousel's, not the product's),
  the **forms and substitutes** (the CSV is the authority, and the
  first pass had them wrong), and the **Indonesian availability
  scores** (a marketplace listing does not support "common").
- **A TOOL BUG was found and fixed by the researcher.** The registry
  helper did not URL-encode its query, so **any term with a space
  failed** with a message that looked like the registry being
